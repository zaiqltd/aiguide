Load shedding does not have to mean your customers hit silence. When the power drops at your premises, the
physical half of your business pauses, but the digital half can stay open, because it does not live in your
shop at all. A WhatsApp AI assistant and your automations run in the cloud, so they keep answering, taking
orders and following up while the lights are off. Here is how that works, and the one thing you still need.

## Why cloud AI keeps working when your power doesn't

The key fact is where the software actually runs. Your AI chatbot does not sit on a computer in your shop;
it lives on a cloud server, somewhere with its own power and backup. So when Eskom takes your block off the
grid, the assistant carries on exactly as before: it answers enquiries on WhatsApp, qualifies leads and
takes orders, day and night, with no interruption at your end. The blackout is local to your premises; the
thing serving your customers is not there.

<Callout variant="fact">
Your chatbot keeps replying during load shedding for the same reason a website you visit does not go down
when your own power cuts: it runs on a server elsewhere, not in your building. The power cut at your shop
never reaches it, so customers get the same fast answers at 2pm or during a stage-6 evening blackout.
</Callout>

## The continuity stack

A handful of cloud-based tools keep the customer-facing side of your business open through an outage:

- **A WhatsApp AI assistant** that answers enquiries and takes orders automatically, on the channel your
  customers already use, without needing power at your shop.
- **Cloud accounting or a CRM** you can reach from your phone, so invoices, customer records and orders are
  all available wherever you are while the power is down.
- **An automated missed-call-to-WhatsApp follow-up,** so a call you cannot take during a blackout turns into
  an instant WhatsApp message that captures the lead instead of losing it.
- **An offline-capable POS or inventory system** that keeps recording sales locally and syncs back up the
  moment the power and connection return.

## What you still need

The honest caveat is connectivity. The cloud tools stay up on their own, but you and your customers still
need to reach them. Your customers manage this themselves: they have charged phones and mobile data during
load shedding, which is exactly why WhatsApp keeps working. On your side, a charged phone with mobile data
keeps you reachable, and a small UPS or power bank for your wifi router or phone bridges the short outages
so you do not drop offline mid-conversation.

<Callout variant="note">
The chatbot needs no power from you. What you are protecting with a power bank or a small router UPS is your
own ability to stay connected, read the conversations and step in when the assistant hands one over. Keep
your phone charged and your data topped up and you are reachable through any stage of load shedding.
</Callout>

## What AI can't fix

Be clear about the line. AI keeps the digital storefront open, not the physical one. The assistant can take
an order during a blackout, but it cannot cook the food, run the workshop, keep the fridge cold or power the
card machine; anything that physically needs electricity at your premises still stops without a generator,
inverter or UPS. Software keeps the conversation and the orders flowing so nothing is lost, but it does not
turn the lights back on. Sell what you can deliver, and use the downtime to confirm and schedule the rest.

## Setting it up

The practical sequence is short:

1. **Put the chatbot on WhatsApp.** Get an AI assistant answering enquiries and taking orders on the channel
   your customers already message you on.
2. **Move the tools that matter to the cloud.** Accounting, customer records and orders should be reachable
   from your phone, not stranded on a machine that loses power.
3. **Add the missed-call automation.** Wire up the missed-call-to-WhatsApp follow-up so no unanswered call
   during an outage becomes a lost customer.
4. **Add a small UPS for the router.** A modest UPS or power bank for your wifi and phone keeps your own side
   of the line up through the short outages.
